## Developing a contact to a least depth

Under the NOAA specification, all contacts identified shall be developed with a
multibeam echo sounder, using the object detection or complete coverage grid
resolution assigned in the project instructions, to determine the least depth of
the contact. The least depth measurement should be determined from a beam within
30 degrees of nadir unless multiple passes were made over the contact.

Where a contact must be developed by single beam echo sounder, dive
investigation may be used to determine depth with a diver's least depth gauge
where practical, and the shoaler of the two methods is the one that stands.

That last rule is the governing principle for a hazard: when two measurements
disagree, the shallower one is used.

## Shoal water

All significant shoals or features found in waters less than 20 metres shall be
developed to complete coverage. Shallow water raises the standard rather than
lowering it, because the consequence of an undetected feature is grounding.

## A wreck is not a cleared site

GICHD lists shipwrecks containing unexploded ordnance as one of its categories
of underwater explosive ordnance contamination, alongside sea mines, torpedoes,
aerial munitions and dumped munitions.

Identifying a contact as a wreck therefore says nothing about whether the site
is safe to approach or dive. Many wrecks carry munitions, bunker fuel and
entanglement hazards, and a wreck classification does not clear any of them.

## Matching a contact against a registry

Compare a contact against the wreck registry on position, least depth, overall
length and beam, orientation on the seabed, and recorded condition. Position
alone is not a match.

A measured least depth shallower than the charted least depth is a navigational
hazard and is reported to the hydrographic authority so the chart can be
updated. An uncharted wreck is reported with position, least depth, dimensions,
orientation, and the survey method and its accuracy.

## Protected sites

Some wrecks are war graves or otherwise legally protected, and disturbance
including artefact recovery may be an offence.
