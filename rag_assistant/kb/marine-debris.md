## How NOAA categorises debris

Items are categorized and counted according to material and then item type. The
material categories are Plastic, Metal, Glass, Rubber, Processed Wood, Fabric,
Custom and Other. Item types sit beneath a material, so an item is placed first
by what it is made of.

Item types relevant to objects that reach the seabed include rope and nets in
synthetic material, rope and nets in natural fibre, buoys and floats, and traps.

## Applying a shoreline taxonomy to sonar contacts

The MDMAP guide is written for shoreline transect surveys, where an item is
picked up and examined. Material is not directly observable in side scan sonar
imagery. Acoustic return relates to density and geometry rather than to polymer
type, so the primary axis of this taxonomy cannot be read off a sonar image.

Use it for vocabulary and for consistency of naming once an object is recovered
or otherwise confirmed. Do not treat a material category as something a
classifier can assign from imagery alone.

## Debris classification is not a hazard clearance

A debris call is a statement about category, not about safety. Three cautions
follow, each grounded in the other documents in this corpus.

Ordnance is found in the same seabed contexts as debris, including plane wrecks,
shipwrecks and munitions dumpsites. A debris field is where ordnance is most
likely to be mistaken for scrap.

Derelict rope and netting is the principal entanglement threat to a towed body,
a thruster and a diver. Do not pass a towed sensor through a net field.

A drum, cylinder or pressure vessel of unknown contents is not debris for the
purposes of this system. It is an unidentified object and is handled under the
unidentified object protocol until an authority identifies it.

## Confidence

A classification below the operator's confidence threshold is treated as
unidentified, not as debris. Debris is the lowest-consequence category available
to the classifier, which makes it the most dangerous default.
