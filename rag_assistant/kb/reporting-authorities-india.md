## Two different channels, and they must not be confused

A discharge of oil or a hazardous and noxious substance goes to the pollution
reporting channel. A suspected explosive object goes to emergency services and
to the navigational warning system. Sending an ordnance report to a pollution
desk delays the response and routes it to people without the authority to act.

## Pollution: the Indian obligation, verbatim in substance

Masters or other persons having charge of ships, and persons having charge of
offshore facilities, involved in an incident shall report without delay and to
the fullest extent possible to the nearest Indian Coast Guard Maritime Rescue
Coordination Centre the particulars of any event on their ship or offshore unit
involving a discharge or probable discharge of oil, of any quantity, in Indian
waters.

The same duty applies to any observed event at sea involving a discharge or
probable discharge of oil, of any quantity, or the presence of oil in Indian
waters, whether or not the reporting vessel was involved.

The Indian Coast Guard publishes a Spill Notification Pro forma for oil and
hazardous and noxious substances, which is the structure a pollution report
should follow.

## Suspected ordnance: what is verified, and what is not

The Canadian Department of National Defence directs a person who encounters
suspected unexploded ordnance to leave the area and call emergency services, and
directs mariners to the Notice to Mariners for further safety instructions if
ordnance is discovered while fishing. That establishes the pattern: emergency
services first, and the navigational warning system for onward dissemination.

INDIAN REPORTING AUTHORITY FOR SUSPECTED ORDNANCE: NOT SPECIFIED.

No source in this corpus names the Indian authority, channel or number for
reporting a suspected mine or item of unexploded ordnance. The Coast Guard
material verified here covers pollution only. Do not substitute the pollution
channel, and do not supply a contact number that is not in the sources.

## What a report carries

Time of detection in UTC. Position as latitude and longitude with the datum and
an accuracy estimate. Depth of water and depth of the object. Object dimensions
and orientation, and how they were derived. Sensor type, frequency, range scale
and towfish height. Classification and confidence, or a statement that the
object is unidentified. The unprocessed sensor record. Vessel identity, the
reporting operator, the vessel's current position and its intentions.

## Sequence

Report first, then hold separation and await direction. A hazard to navigation
is broadcast to other mariners by the authority, not by the detecting vessel.
