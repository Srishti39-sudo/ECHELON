## What it is

Unexploded explosive ordnance is the term for military explosives that were used
but failed to function properly. Underwater unexploded ordnance is found in both
inland and offshore waters and results from former ranges, plane wrecks,
shipwrecks and munitions dumpsites.

Unexploded ordnance may still be dangerous even if it has been in the water for
many years. Age and immersion are not evidence of safety.

## The operator rule

The Canadian Department of National Defence states it in three lines.

Do not touch it. Remember the location and leave the area. Call emergency
services.

The same source states that if unexploded ordnance is disturbed it could explode
and cause serious injury or death, and that commercial and sport divers,
fishermen and mariners should be aware there is a chance of encountering it at
sea. Mariners are directed to the Notice to Mariners for further safety
instructions in the event that unexploded ordnance is discovered while fishing.

That routing matters. An ordnance encounter goes to the navigational warning
system and to emergency services. It is not a pollution report.

## Safe distance is not a fixed number

There is no published universal standoff distance for an underwater object of
unknown type, and this system will not supply one.

The reason is mechanical rather than a gap in the record. IMAS derives a danger
area radius from the explosive weight involved:

    R = 634 x (AUW)^(1/6)     public have access to the immediate area
    R = 444 x (AUW)^(1/6)     access controlled, no public or animals present

R is the range in metres. AUW is the All Up Weight of the demolition in
kilograms, which includes the net explosive content of the munitions, the weight
of their casings and fuzing systems, and the weight of any donor charges. IMAS
worked example: an All Up Weight of 1500 kg gives R of 2145 m under the public
access formula.

The All Up Weight of an unidentified object on a sonar image is unknown, so the
radius is not computable from a detection. IMAS also states the conservative
default directly: if there is any doubt about whether access is controlled, the
public access formula should be used.

REQUIRED STANDOFF FOR AN UNIDENTIFIED OBJECT: NOT SPECIFIED. It is not
specifiable without the explosive content, which a sonar contact does not give.

## Disposal

DISPOSAL METHOD: NOT SPECIFIED in this corpus, deliberately.

Disposal and render-safe procedures are planned and executed by the explosive
ordnance disposal authority with jurisdiction. No survey operator, diver,
fisherman or vessel crew performs them. This document exists to get an operator
to withdraw and to hand the object to that authority, and it carries no
procedure that could be mistaken for authorisation to act.
