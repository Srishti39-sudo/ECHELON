## Standing assumption

An underwater object that cannot be positively identified is treated as
potentially explosive and potentially hazardous until a qualified authority
identifies it. Absence of a classification is not evidence of safety.

This follows from two verified facts. Unexploded ordnance may still be dangerous
after many years in the water, and it is found in offshore waters as a result of
former ranges, plane wrecks, shipwrecks and munitions dumpsites, which are the
same seabed contexts a survey encounters. And a sonar contact is a measurement
of returned sound rather than an identification of what returned it.

## Immediate actions

Do not touch it. Remember the location and leave the area. Call emergency
services.

Applied to a survey platform, that means stop closing on the object, hold or
increase the current separation, and do not close to improve the image. Record
the time, position, depth and sensor settings at the moment of detection, and
preserve the unprocessed sonar record before any processing.

Do not lift, drag, net, anchor near, or recover the object. Do not attempt
identification by physical contact of any kind, including with a manipulator arm
or a remotely operated vehicle.

## Separation distance

REQUIRED STANDOFF: NOT SPECIFIED.

No source in this corpus publishes a universal safe distance for an underwater
object of unknown type. The danger radius depends on the All Up Weight of the
explosive involved, which is unknown for an unidentified object. See the
unexploded ordnance document for the IMAS formulas and the reasoning.

Hold the separation at which the object was detected or greater. Do not reduce
it to improve a classification.

## Identity

IDENTITY: NOT SPECIFIED.

Nearest known objects may be offered as ranked possibilities, each paired with
what would confirm it and what would rule it out. A similarity ranking is not an
identification and must never be presented as one.

## Confirmation, when an authority directs it

NOAA develops every identified contact with a second sensor, using a multibeam
echo sounder to determine the least depth, taken from a beam within 30 degrees
of nadir unless multiple passes were made. Confirmation is by a second sensor or
a second geometry at range. It is never by approach or contact.

## Escalation

Every unidentified object is escalated to a qualified expert. The object remains
unidentified until that expert rules. Do not resume the survey line through the
area until the responsible authority responds.

## Marking

Do not mark the position with a physical buoy or float unless the authority
directs it. A surface marker advertises the position to third parties and can
draw an uninformed vessel onto the hazard. Position is passed to the authority,
which issues a navigational warning if one is warranted.
