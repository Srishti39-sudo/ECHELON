## What a sonar contact can and cannot establish

Nothing in these sources describes a way to identify human remains from an
acoustic return. The obligations below attach to a site where remains are known
or suspected to be present. A classifier output naming a human is not such a
finding, and the sources give no basis for treating it as one.

## The standing obligation

States Parties shall ensure that proper respect is given to all human remains
located in maritime waters (Article 2, paragraph 9).

Human remains, together with their archaeological and natural context, fall
within the definition of underwater cultural heritage where they have been under
water for at least 100 years (Article 1, paragraph 1(a)(i)). Vessels and
aircraft, their cargo and other contents, are covered by the same definition
(Article 1, paragraph 1(a)(ii)).

## Do not disturb

Activities directed at underwater cultural heritage shall avoid the unnecessary
disturbance of human remains or venerated sites (Annex, Rule 5).

The preservation in situ of underwater cultural heritage shall be considered as
the first option before allowing or engaging in any activities directed at this
heritage (Article 2, paragraph 5).

Activities must use non-destructive techniques and survey methods in preference
to recovery of objects. Where excavation or recovery is necessary for scientific
study or for the ultimate protection of the heritage, the methods used must be
as non-destructive as possible (Annex, Rule 4).

Responsible non-intrusive access to observe or document in situ is encouraged,
except where such access is incompatible with protection and management
(Article 2, paragraph 10).

## Who may work on such a site

Activities directed at underwater cultural heritage shall only be undertaken
under the direction and control of, and in the regular presence of, a qualified
underwater archaeologist with scientific competence appropriate to the project
(Annex, Rule 22).

A survey vessel that has detected a contact is not conducting such an activity
and is not authorised to begin one.

## Reporting a discovery

A State Party shall require that when its national, or a vessel flying its flag,
discovers underwater cultural heritage located in its exclusive economic zone or
on its continental shelf, the national or the master of the vessel shall report
that discovery to it (Article 9, paragraph 1(a)).

## Military wrecks

Sunken military craft owned by the United States remain the property of the
United States regardless of their location or the passage of time, and may not
be disturbed without permission from the U.S. Navy. Such craft and their
contents often serve as war graves, carry unexploded ordnance, and contain oil
or other hazardous materials. It is the policy of the Department of the Navy
that its sunken military craft remain in place and undisturbed.

A wreck may therefore be a grave site and an explosive hazard at the same time,
and the two obligations do not cancel each other out.

## What these sources do not specify

These sources give NO standoff distance for a site where human remains are or
may be present. NOT SPECIFIED.

These sources name NO Indian authority for reporting human remains found at sea.
NOT SPECIFIED. India is not a State Party to the 2001 Convention, and the
reporting duty in Article 9 is a duty of States Parties.

These sources contain NO procedure for recovering, handling or identifying human
remains. Nothing here authorises an operator to attempt any of them.
