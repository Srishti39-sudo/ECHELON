## What the sources establish, and what they do not

Abandoned, lost or otherwise discarded fishing gear is abbreviated ALDFG. India's
National Action Plan on Marine Plastic Litter from Sea-based Sources (February
2024) states that ALDFG including nets, lines, etc., also contribute to marine
plastic pollution, and that entanglement of organisms, especially in net fragment
litter or 'ghost fishing gear', can cause starvation, suffocation, laceration,
infection, reduced reproductive success and mortality.

NATIONAL GHOST-GEAR REPORTING HOTLINE OR CHANNEL: NOT FOUND in these sources.

In the National Action Plan's baseline data table, the item "Identify and list
active promotion, and existing practices reporting mechanisms for lost fishing
gear, if any" is marked "In preparation", against "Dept. of Fisheries, MoFAH&D/
Dept. of Fisheries, State Govt./UTs". The country assessment in the same plan
answers "No." to whether the Fishing Acts/Codes include provisions to prevent
and/or reduce abandoned, lost or otherwise discarded fishing gear.

The offices named below are therefore the offices the sources give responsibility
to for a situation. None of them is stated to be a ghost-gear reporting desk, and
no phone number, email or form for a ghost-gear report appears in any source.

## Lost or found fishing gear: the fisheries authorities

The Government of India participates in the IMO-FAO GloLitter Partnerships project
"through Department of Fisheries (DoF), Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry &
Dairying (MFAHD), as the lead agency". The Fishery Survey of India (FSI) is named
as the GloLitter lead agency, and FSI submits biannual reports to the Department
of Fisheries.

The plan states: "Fisheries is a state subject, at regional levels, fisheries
development is governed by respective State Fisheries Departments. All the Coastal
States and Union Territories regulate their marine fisheries through Marine
Fishing Regulation Acts (MFRAs)."

On enforcement areas, the plan states that within the territorial waters of 12 NM
enforcement is by the respective coastal States through State Marine Fishing
Regulation Acts, and that in the area beyond 12 NM and within the EEZ "it is the
responsibility of the Ministry of Defence through Coast Guard and Navy". This is
stated about fisheries enforcement, not about receiving ghost-gear reports.

## ALDFG actions in the National Action Plan, and who is responsible

The plan assigns these actions, with the responsible authority as printed:

- Action 5.1.2, sensitize marking of fishing gear based on the FAO Voluntary
  Guidelines on the Marking of Fishing Gear: "DoF. Central / State Govts" with
  "Central and State Fisheries Depts., Fishermen Associations, Net Manufacturers/
  Importers Associations".
- Action 5.1.4, "Sensitization of fishers on ALDFG reporting": "DoF, FSI. Central /
  State Govts."
- Action 5.2.6, incentive based schemes "routed through State Fisheries Department
  for collection, and surrender of damaged, unrepairable nets/ALDFG": "FSI, Dept.
  of Fisheries, MFAH&D, Central/State/UT Fisheries Departments, Fishers, Fishermen
  organizations". Its progress indicator is a periodic report of the number of
  cases of reported ALDFG and data on locations of loss.
- Action 5.2.7, RFID or smart buoy technology for tracking of gear and FAD
  retrieval: "FSI, CIFT, CIFNET, ICAR, Central and State government Depts/ other
  Institutes".

All of these run 2024 to 2026. They are planned actions, not a reporting procedure
in force.

## Retrieval already under way

The plan states: "The Fishery Survey of India (FSI) survey vessels are actively
retrieving the abandoned, lost, or otherwise discarded fishing gears (ALDFG) and
other SBMPL encountered during their voyages. Several drifting and entangled
longlines, and abandoned fishing nets were being collected and brought back to
shore."

It also sets an expected outcome of removal of sea-based marine plastic litter
from Indian seas "to an approximate quantity of 10 tonnes in a year for four
years".

## What the FAO guidelines say a reporting regime should contain

India's plan adopts the FAO Voluntary Guidelines on the Marking of Fishing Gear as
the basis for gear marking. The guidelines are voluntary and international; they
describe what a State should set up, not an Indian procedure.

States should encourage anyone who finds deployed gear without appropriate marks
to report it to the relevant authorities (paragraph 33). Reports of lost gear
should be made in a timely manner "to the flag State, to the relevant authority
that issued any relevant gear mark, and, if relevant, to the coastal State in
whose jurisdiction the loss of the fishing gear occurred" (paragraph 35).

The relevant authority should keep a register of gear reported as found,
abandoned, lost or otherwise discarded, with, where available: gear ownership;
type and characteristics of the gear; any gear marks and identifiers; date, time,
position of loss or retrieval and depth of water; reason for loss; weather
conditions; any other relevant information including entrapment of endangered,
threatened or protected species; and status, retrieved or not retrieved
(paragraph 36).

## Recovery priorities in the FAO guidelines

Owners and operators should make every reasonable effort to retrieve ALDFG; if
recovery fails, it should be reported to the relevant authority (paragraph 39).
Recovery should be undertaken with due regard to human safety and the damage
retrieval may do to the marine environment and habitat (paragraph 40).

Priority should be given to the retrieval of ALDFG that (paragraph 41):

- presents a hazard to the navigation of surface and sub-surface vessels, or to
  fishing operations;
- poses a significant adverse impact to critical, vulnerable or otherwise
  sensitive habitats;
- poses an entanglement, entrapment or ingestion threat to marine wildlife or has
  a potential of ghost fishing.

Recovered ALDFG should be recycled or disposed of responsibly on land, with port
reception facilities in accordance with MARPOL Annex V (paragraph 44).

## Ghost gear posing a navigation or propeller hazard

The FAO guidelines state: "Recognizing that ALDFG may be a danger to navigation,
States should encourage the owner/operator of the ALDFG to immediately warn other
vessels in the vicinity, giving details of the gear and its last known position.
The relevant authority should use the most effective means to give a general
warning to other vessels." (paragraph 38).

In India, the National Hydrographic Office website lists among the mandates of
the Chief Hydrographer: "Coordinator of NAVAREA VIII, as designated by the IHO."
and "National Coordinator for NAVTEX services." Its stated maritime safety service
is to "Coordinate NAVAREA VIII warnings and NAVTEX services along the Indian coast
for timely dissemination of maritime safety information to mariners."

The IMO list of NAVAREA coordinators (COMSAR.1/Circ.58/Rev.1, 31 October 2018)
names the NAVAREA VIII coordinator as: Joint Director of Hydrography, Maritime
Safety Information Services (MSIS), National Hydrographic Office, 107-A, Rajpur
Road, P.B. No. 75, Dehradun, Uttarakhand - 248 001, India. It prints the email
msis-inho@navy.gov.in "(For urgent NAVAREA promulgation)". These details are as
printed in 2018 and may have changed.

## Reporting a danger to navigation to the National Hydrographic Office

The National Hydrographic Office publishes an online Abridged Hydrographic Note
"(For Reporting Navigational Dangers/ Changes observed at Sea by Mariners)". Its
fields are: general locality; chart affected, edition number, ENC affected and
latest update held; date/time of observation; details of changes/dangers
observed; position of the danger as latitude and longitude; datum (WGS 84,
Everest or local); name and address of sender or ship, fax, email and phone; and
accompanying plots or photographs.

The fuller Hydrographic Note I.H. 102 includes "Nav. Dangers" as an object of
changes observed, and asks for the position method (DGPS, GPS, radar, other) and
the datum used.

The sources do NOT state that a submerged or floating net, as such, will be
promulgated as a navigational warning. That decision rests with the authority.
The detecting vessel reports; it does not broadcast.

## Ghost gear inside or near the Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park

The Ramanathapuram District Administration page for Forest, Environment & Climate
Change states that the Ramanathapuram Forest, Environment & Climate Change
Department (printed "Envrionment") oversees the following protected areas,
including "1. Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park" and "2. Gulf of Mannar
Biosphere Reserve". Its listed activities include "Protection of Wildlife" and
"Habitat Restoration such as Coral reef, Sea grass, Mangrove, etc."

The same page states that the 21 islands and the surrounding shallow coastal
waters covering 560 sq km between Rameswaram and Tuticorin were declared as Gulf
of Mannar Marine National Park vide G.O.Ms.No.962, Forests and Fisheries Dept.,
10th September 1986, and that the Gulf of Mannar Biosphere Reserve covers 10,500
sq km between Rameswaram and Kanyakumari with the Marine National Park as its
core area.

Contact details as printed on that page head the list with "Wildlife Warden,
Ramanthapuram. Mobile:- 9445468452" (spelling as printed; the Wildlife Warden,
Ramanathapuram), followed by the Range Forest Officers (RFO) for Ramanathapuram,
Mandapam, Keelkarai and Tuticorin. The page names these as department contacts. It
does not name them as the channel for ghost-gear reports.

## Ghost gear in the Palk Bay Dugong Conservation Reserve

G.O.(Ms).No.165, Environment, Climate Change and Forests (FR.5) Department,
Government of Tamil Nadu, dated 21.09.2022, declares the "Dugong Conservation
Reserve" in Palk Bay under section 36A(1) of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972,
from 21st September 2022. The reserve is the coastal waters of Thanjavur and
Pudukkottai districts from Adirampattinam to Ammapattinam, bounded 10 kilometres
from the coastal boundary towards the sea; total extent 448.34 sq. kilometres.

The notification was proposed by the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests and
Chief Wildlife Warden, and the order is addressed among others to "The District
Forest Officer, Thanjavur and Pudukkottai". The notification states that dugong
numbers have come down drastically "Due to uncontrolled fishing carried out in the
past and also due to reduction in their grazing area".

MANAGING AUTHORITY OF THE DUGONG CONSERVATION RESERVE: NOT STATED in the G.O. The
offices above are named as proposer and as addressees, not as the manager.

## Ghost gear near Gahirmatha and Odisha sea turtle nesting areas

Wildlife Odisha's record for Gahirmatha Marine Sanctuary lists: forest division
"Mangrove Forest Division (Wildlife), Rajnagar"; district "Kendrapara & Bhadrak";
notified area "1435.00 sq.km"; notification date 27 September 1997; and describes
it as the "World's largest nesting beach for Olive Ridley turtles". The same
record's descriptive text contains travel and ecosystem statements that are not
relied on here.

A Government of Odisha, Forest, Environment & Climate Change Department
notification dated 12 March 2026 constitutes a "State Coordination Committee" to
function as a State-level institutional mechanism for sea turtle conservation in
the State, chaired by the "PCCF (Wildlife) & Chief Wildlife Warden, Odisha". Its
members include a representative from Fisheries and ARD Department, Odisha; from
Coastal Securities, Odisha Police; and from "Coast Guard, DHQ-7 (Odisha),
Paradip". The Committee "shall formulate & implement a State specific Standard
Operating Procedure (SOP) for sea turtle conservation, addressing key causes of
turtle mortality including use of trawlers and trawl nets, entanglement in nets,
poaching, hatchling mortality, and loss of nesting grounds."

SEASONAL FISHING BAN NEAR ODISHA NESTING BEACHES: NOT VERIFIED from an official
source in this corpus. The SOP the Committee is to formulate is not in the corpus.

## Wildlife found entangled in gear

No source here names an Indian authority or procedure for reporting a turtle,
dugong, dolphin or other animal found entangled in ghost gear. NOT SPECIFIED.

What the sources do say: dugongs are protected under the Wild Life (Protection)
Act, 1972 (G.O.(Ms).No.165); the Odisha State Coordination Committee is to address
entanglement in nets as a cause of turtle mortality; the Ramanathapuram Forest,
Environment & Climate Change Department lists protection of wildlife among its
activities; and the FAO guidelines include entrapment of endangered, threatened or
protected species in the information a lost-gear register should hold.

Nothing here authorises a survey crew to free an entangled animal or to dive on a
net. Recovery is to be undertaken with due regard to human safety (FAO guidelines,
paragraph 40).
